Someone referred us a lead this week, an intro over email to a buyer who could use our services. Here's the exact playbook I use to level up what everyone on that email expects:

1. Urgency - while I can't always do it, I try to reply to the buyer before they reply to me.

2. Two seconds of Show Me You Know Me first - who are they, who do we know, have they worked for clients*, where do they live, what have they posted about, how can I connect with them better in my reply.

*If they know other people who have hired us, I'm prioritizing that for additional creditability

3. My reply structure:

Thanks to the introductions
Some SMYKM notes/connections
Happy to find time - send me some dates/time (no calendar link, court them)
Thanks again to the referral, say I'm moving referrer to bcc*

*Sometimes reps only reply to the buyer, which leaves your referrer in the dark (and annoyed). I also like to thank them twice in the email vs. just at the top + "moving you to BCC" which feels a little rushed/greedy, IMO.

4. BCC myself on the email - not everyone replies and sometimes we need a follow up nudge. I don't want the opp to fall through the cracks, and this isn't an email I send as part of a cadence.

4. Another thank you email to the referral source under separate cover. "Really appreciate you thinking of us and sticking your neck out for us..."

5. An update to the referrer as things progress. This can be once mid-way if it's a larger ENT deal that will take time, or only at the end when you win/lose, if a short sales cycle. Closing the loop speaks volumes here.

6. If we win the deal, we're sending a handwritten note to the referrer or a gift that...you guessed it...Show Me You Know Me's them. It doesn't need to cost much - a book goes a long way - but it's one extra touch of gratitude for revenue you wouldn't have had without them.

All of these steps are important for not only mapping you out as a completely different kind of seller, but of proving to the referrer that they can continue to gamble their reputation on you with more referrals.

## Post 2
URL: https://www.linkedin.com/posts/samsalesli_samsales-activity-7470894373103304704-M-OU

One of my favorite workshops we run is all around ghosting in the sales cycle. Here are two examples of what we recently uncovered and how you can run your own.

How To:

Limit the sessions to eight reps
Each one brings a deal that's stalled
They bring the emails they sent, the history, the call notes

We dissect it all and tell you why they likely ghosted, how to course-correct for next time, and what to do now to at least get a reply.

Bonus: run this for your team, but invite two peer leaders to do the dissecting. You'll be amazed what a different perspective can bring.

Two recent scenarios:

1. A client sent a Show Me You Know Me email. Two weeks later, they followed up to ask for time. The response: No, I'm on PTO these next two weeks.

The takeaways:

- Email two should never come that late. <48 hours after email one.
- I asked if they'd noticed the time stamp of the reply. It was about an hour after the second email went out.
- The rep's leader was recently at an event with the buyer's leader

Imagine if we'd caught this sooner. Imagine if email two had gone out 44 hours later, if the buyer had replied in the same time window. We'd potentially have gotten the meeting before PTO, started the process, and have gotten details the leaders could have discussed at the event rather than still working to book time in.

2. A buyer showed interest in a platform. Rep replies, feature-dumps, silence in return. The "just following up" emails ensue, and now we're stuck.

The easiest way to get unstuck here is to simply own the mistake with a script like this:

Mark, I fear I didn't do justice to how our platform can help, mostly because I wrote you an email telling you what we do vs. what we solve. For your specific use case, here are three challenges I can help you with... If the window hasn't closed to win your business, I know I can make great use of your time, and would so appreciate the consideration.

The reply? "Your self-awareness won me over, and I was candidly stuck because your competition wrote me almost the same first email you did. Happy to make time to talk."

Small incremental catches and changes can impact deals, but the best part is that coaching to this nitty gritty stuff will them impact every other motion that rep, and those listening, will take.

"LinkedIn recommends that we do more video, so we do, and they flop. Why would they recommend something that doesn't seem to work?"

That...is a great question.

Here's the thing, video can be effective in three ways:

1. You're a notable exec celeb and people want to hear whatever you share (Jon Gray @ Blackstone)

2. You're not one but built a following and people love hearing you (influencers in general)

3. None of the above, but it humanizes you + warms buyers up to you

What the recommendation misses is an understanding of the creators and the audience on LinkedIn specifically.

We might be in a TikTok + reels era, but those making content here, especially at an executive level, aren't adept with that style, nor does it fit their executive persona.

So what might stop the scroll on another platform, doesn't here, because it lacks the scroll-stopping words or style.

Further? A video requires the viewer to learn at the pace of the video, not at theirs or with the ability to skim, learn on their terms; so, they move along.

Further? 90%+ of video is watched on mute, so if you're not captioning your content, you're losing a lot of your dwell time.

Further? You may create content without any media training, which can actually damage your brand vs. help it.

Video CAN work well for specific use cases, and you should have some in your featured section to let buyers/recruiters easily find it, but a stop-the-scroll text hook + an image or infographic will work over video, for the great majority of content creators on this platform.
