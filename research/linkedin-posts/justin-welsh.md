## Post 3
URL: https://www.linkedin.com/posts/justinwelsh_i-got-fired-three-times-before-i-turned-28-activity-7470441746372472833-gAHX

I got fired three times before I turned 28.

Same industry.
Three lame, podunk towns.
Same reasonable logic to get another job.

I was the biggest loser in my friend group.

Then my mom stumbled upon a job on a Craigslist ad and sent it to me.

It was a door-to-door sales job in NYC at a company nobody had ever heard of in a city I definitely couldn't afford.

I had a much safer, much higher-paying offer sitting right in front of me back home in Cleveland.

Every person told me to take that job.
It was "reasonable," they said.

I moved to NYC instead.

Slept on a futon next to a rack that held my entire wardrobe inside of my childhood friend's living room.

Nobody told me to choose New York.
I went anyway.

Unreasonable.

Almost every good thing in my life traces back to that one unreasonable choice.

Here's what I've learned since:

Reasonable choices compound, and each choice makes the next one easier.

Take the promotion.
Move for the job.
Buy the bigger house.

Every link is defensible.
Everyone nods along.
Thumbs up.

Then, 40 years later, you often wake up inside a life you don't like.

One you never chose.

The unreasonable choices get loud warnings.

What if you fail?
What if it doesn't work out?
What if people think you're crazy?

The reasonable choices get decades of applause.

Nobody warns you about that one.

I wrote a full essay about it.

The reasonable chain of choices, how it builds, and the one question that breaks it.
