What actually IS an autonomous business?

I started noticing the term "autonomous business" when Amos Bar-Joseph entered RB2B's space and made a splash with an incredibly compelling narrative about AI and building the startup of the future.

I remember reading posts from 2 years ago like it was yesterday…

He had this exciting way of positioning himself as a true AI expert, creating a totally new type of startup.

"Just three guys and an army of 30 AI agents", "scaling to $30m ARR" (my favorite credibility line ever).

I - along with thousands of others - read these posts and WANTED all of these agents.

But I also had this feeling that the way they were going about it, Swan AI wasn't ever going to be truly autonomous … Nor was that the goal.

These guys were actually working around the clock with these agents, and planned to continue to do so indefinitely.

And by the way - there is nothing wrong with that.

In fact, I'd go as far as to say if you are NOT committed to working around the clock indefinitely, you will almost certainly NOT be successful.

But back to my original question …

Is 3 guys working around the clock ACTUALLY an autonomous business?

My answer would be "no".

I have a fairly un-evolved definition of an "autonomous business" …

My definition is "a business that runs itself without any humans."

Ironically, when we started RB2B, that was the ultimate goal, but never became part of the story. We designed the entire machine to be simple enough to where all customer interaction could be handled by AI, and our cheap acquisition channel (my thought leadership) helped us price so low that people didn't expect more than that from us.

Fast forward 2 years...

We refuse to add anything beyond a pixel and a push, AI handles over 99% of our total customer tickets, and our CSAT is in the high 90's.

What's even crazier is that we stopped working on RB2B six weeks ago to pursue MoltSets.

What do I mean by that?

- Tate has not touched the code in six weeks (true story)
- Robb spends under 15min/day answering AI-escalated tickets
- I post 3x/week on LinkedIn, ironically NOT about RB2B

… And we're currently growing at over 7%/mo, will cross $10m ARR soon, and it's taking under 90min of human time per week.

Based on my primitive definition - a business that runs itself w/o humans - RB2B is the closest thing to "autonomous" that I've ever heard of.

How would you define an autonomous business?
What are other examples?

## Post 3

Founder: I've got 35 AI agents making me 100x more productive!

Me: But you're still on Zoom calls 9 hours a day...

Founder: [nods]

Me: You're missing the point of AI

Founder: What do you mean?

Me: You're trying to bend AI to fit into your existing business. And yeah, you will see some incredible gains come from that. But it's not enough.

Founder: So what's the problem?

Me: If you rebuilt your product, pricing, and go-to-market around AI… it could run your entire front line. Every customer conversation. Every interaction. You'd be free to focus on SUPER-high-leverage stuff. Product, brand, growth.

Founder: That SOUNDS nice, but our product requires a sales call to get people properly educated and onboarded.

Me: You're still missing the point. If your product, pricing, and positioning is simple enough, AI can handle ALMOST every customer interaction. We proved that with RB2B.

Founder: But our product is more complex than yours. It has to be.

Me: That's what every SaaS founder says right before they get out-executed by some YC kid with an AI-native business. Over the next few years, the old guard will stall out, while a new generation of truly AI-native companies emerge who design their product and go-to-market strategies in a way where every human minute spent ACTUALLY gets amplified by 100x.

Founder: What does that even look like?

Me: AI has made the 3-man, $10m ARR SaaS a reality. How? If you're not on calls all day, you can spend your time on MUCH higher leverage activity like product, pricing, and growth.

Founder: Yeah, but we aren't RB2B. So what should I do?

Me: Stop asking, 'How can AI help us do more of what we already do?' Start asking, 'What would my business look like if AI could do almost all of it?' Design that version. Then build toward it.

Founder: That's kind of terrifying.

Me: It should be. Because if you don't, you're screwed.

Founder: And if I do?

Me: You win. $10M ARR. A couple FTEs. The FU money of SaaS.
