## Post 1
URL: https://www.linkedin.com/posts/michellejraymond_image-carousel-activity-7469992564104876032-Tc08

I've been talking about The Power of Two - building your company brand and employee brands together for better results for around 5 years now. This new report from Metricool and Favikon backs up my theory (AGAIN!).

Here are my thoughts on the report findings in no particular order -

1) If your Page is one of the 93% that didn't grow last year, we need to talk 😂
2) Here's my pretend surprise face that Profiles get more engagement. 😲
3) Want people to comment on your post? Ask a question or tell them to comment. Your audience aren't mind readers 🔮
4) "Brands get shares. People get replies. You need both." No arguments from me. That's employees supporting Page content and actual people having conversations. Doesn't mean you can't get comments on Pages - just ask Manychat.
5) I've been banging on about the power of polls for so long now - give them a crack and let me know how you go.
6) Carousels - have their place in the mix, but they aren't the wonder performers like they were back in 2022. They are just getting measured differently. Every time someone swipes to see the next slide, that counts as a click. So, of course, if you have one image, you max out at one click.
7) These reports are not to be confused with LinkedIn strategy!

These are always fun to look at and can be interesting to see trends that might pop up when we look back at data.

But ever noticed that reports generated by different teams often conflict with each other?

My best tip - create content your customer wants/needs and enjoys.

What's your best tip?

## Post 3
URL: https://www.linkedin.com/posts/michellejraymond_linkedin-success-takes-more-than-being-part-activity-7470329669544906752-hgUW

LinkedIn success takes more than being part of a big brand with a Company Page that has 2M followers, LinkedIn Ads budget, and thousands of employees. In this case, the real wins have come from the hard work done behind the scenes.

When Molly Hopkins joined CBRE, she was the first social media hire in the US. No team. No playbook.

Her added bonus - over 350 branded social media accounts running pretty much as a free-for-all. 😂

What turned that chaos into one of the best-run LinkedIn ecosystems I've seen was the unglamorous stuff:
✅ 2 years dedicated to process building and putting everything in writing so she's not having the same conversation 10 times.
✅ Setting strict criteria for what makes the global Company Page.
✅ Learning to say "no, but..." and always offering a better home for the idea.
✅ A simple 4-slide deck showing teams what running a page actually takes before they create another one.

Processes aren't the boring part of social media.

They're the reason the exciting parts work.

This week on the Social Media for B2B Growth podcast, Molly and I dig into how global B2B brands balance Company Pages, paid, organic and employee advocacy.

The whole conversation is gold for anyone managing LinkedIn behind the scenes.

What's the one process that's saved your sanity as a social media or marketing manager?
